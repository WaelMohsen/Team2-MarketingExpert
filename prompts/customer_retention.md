Target: Customer Retention

What it means:
- Keep existing customers buying again and reduce the number who stop purchasing.

Primary objective:
- Reduce churn and increase repeat purchase value.

Primary KPIs (use the provided data when available):

| KPI | How to interpret | Typical good range (context-dependent) |
|-----|------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Retained Customers | Higher is better (more customers stayed active) | Up and to the right |
| Churn Rate | Lower is better (fewer customers dropping off) | Often 2–8% monthly in many DTC/consumer categories |
| Retention Rate | Higher is better (the flip-side of churn) | Often 92–98% monthly if churn is 2–8% |
| Repeat Purchases Per Year | Higher indicates stronger habit/need | Varies widely by product (2–12+) |

Secondary signals (use as supporting evidence, not the main KPI):
- Customer Satisfaction Score: a leading indicator for future churn risk
- Average order value and revenue trend: tells you how much retention is worth
- Retained customer share: how much of your customer base is returning vs newly acquired

Interpretation guidance:
- Churn rising + retained customers falling: urgent retention issue (value/product/experience mismatch or weak lifecycle messaging)
- Churn stable but retained customers flat: retention ceiling (you’re not expanding the repeat buyer base)
- Repeat purchases/year low: customers like the offer but don’t form a habit (need replenishment, bundles, subscriptions, reminders)
- Satisfaction score falling: churn risk is likely to rise next (fix experience and messaging early)

Red flags:
- Churn rate meaningfully above your normal baseline
- Retained customers declining despite steady spend and traffic
- Repeat purchases per year trending down
- High acquisition success but weak retained customer share (leaky bucket)

Action direction (recommend the single highest-leverage move first):
- Win-back: targeted offers and reminders for lapsed customers
- Lifecycle messaging: post-purchase education, usage tips, replenishment reminders
- Loyalty: simple points/perks, VIP tiers for frequent buyers
- Reduce friction: shipping/returns clarity, product quality fixes, support response time
- Segment-specific offers: different messages for first-time buyers vs repeat buyers

Reasoning mode:
- Think step-by-step internally before answering.
- Do not reveal internal reasoning; return only the final structured answer.

Few-shot examples (style guidance):
Example 1
- Signals: Churn rising, retained customers dropping, satisfaction score slipping.
- Good final direction: "Core issue is customer experience is not meeting expectations. Fix top friction points and launch a win-back flow for recent churned customers."

Example 2
- Signals: Churn stable, retained customers flat, repeat purchases per year low.
- Good final direction: "Core issue is customers aren’t forming a repeat habit. Add replenishment reminders, bundles, and a simple loyalty incentive to drive a second purchase."
